# ETFM Reset Dashboard — Client Portal Upgrade
## Complete Content Guide & Implementation Reference

---

### PHILOSOPHY

This is not a course. There are no modules to complete, no videos to sit through, no passive content to consume. This is a personal financial operating system — a calm command center built around your real life, your real pressure points, and your real goals.

Every section of this dashboard is designed to feel guided, intentional, and software-like. The experience should feel like walking into a room where someone has already thought through every detail and laid out exactly what you need — in the right order, at the right time.

**Design Principle:** Calm over clever. Clarity over volume. Action over information.

---

### TONE GUIDE

- **Grounded.** Speak from the floor up. No hype, no promises, no superlatives.
- **Advisory.** The voice of someone who has seen this pattern before and knows what works.
- **Stabilizing.** Every word should reduce anxiety, not create it.
- **Encouraging.** Not motivational. Not cheerleading. Just honest forward momentum.
- **Calm.** Never urgent. Never alarm. Structure creates calm — and so should the language.
- **Practical.** Specific over general. Concrete over abstract. Always.
- **Never hype.** No "crush it," "transform your life," or "unlock your potential."
- **Never shame.** No language that implies they should already know this or have done better.
- **Never corporate.** No jargon, no passive voice, no bureaucratic phrasing.

## SECTION 2 — YOUR PRIORITY AREAS

*Placement: First full section below the Control Center.*

---

### Section Header

**Your Priority Areas**

### Purpose Statement

> These are the areas where your financial system currently has the most friction. They are not failures — they are signals. Each one points to a specific structural gap that the systems in this dashboard are designed to address. Start where the pressure is highest.

*Design note: Display purpose statement in muted body text below the section header. No bold. No emphasis. Let it breathe.*

---

### Priority Categories

*Implementation instruction: Display as toggle cards or expandable rows. Each category should be selectable — when a client marks a category as a priority, it surfaces related tools and system builders from other sections of the dashboard. This creates a personalized experience without requiring a quiz or onboarding flow.*

---

#### Priority 1 — Financial Reactivity

**What it means:**
Your financial decisions are being driven by pressure, emotion, or circumstance rather than by intention and structure. You find yourself spending in response to stress, making fast decisions you later regret, or avoiding financial decisions until they become emergencies.

**Why it creates pressure:**
Reactive financial behavior is expensive — not just in dollars, but in time and mental energy. Every decision made under pressure costs more than the same decision made from a place of clarity. Reactivity compounds over time into patterns that feel impossible to break.

**What this dashboard addresses:**
The Decision Pause System and the Priority Structure System were built specifically for this. Together, they interrupt the reactive cycle and replace it with a decision-making framework that works even when you're stressed.

**Mark this as a priority if:**
You frequently spend money impulsively, avoid looking at your finances when things feel tight, or feel like your financial decisions are happening to you rather than being made by you.

---

#### Priority 2 — Disorganization

**What it means:**
Your financial life lacks a clear structure. Money comes in, money goes out, and the system holding it all together is either nonexistent or unclear. You may have multiple accounts with no defined purpose, bills scattered across payment methods, or no reliable picture of where things stand at any given moment.

**Why it creates pressure:**
Disorganization is not a personality trait — it is a structural problem. When your financial system has no architecture, every decision requires full mental effort from scratch. Nothing is automated. Nothing is predictable. The pressure never lifts because the system never holds.

**What this dashboard addresses:**
The Weekly Financial Awareness System creates immediate visibility. The Financial Calendar, Bill Organization Template, and Priority Planner provide the structural foundation. The Weekly Reset Routine holds it together week over week.

**Mark this as a priority if:**
You often don't know exactly how much money you have, your bills feel unpredictable, or you feel like your finances are scattered across too many places to manage clearly.

---

#### Priority 3 — Inconsistent Planning

**What it means:**
You have intentions — but they don't consistently translate into action. You start plans, budgets, or savings goals with momentum, then lose traction. The restart cycle is familiar: clarity followed by drift, followed by starting over.

**Why it creates pressure:**
Inconsistent planning is almost never a motivation problem. It is a system problem. When a plan requires sustained willpower to maintain, it will eventually fail — because willpower is a finite resource. The solution is not more discipline. It is a simpler, more repeatable system that runs even when motivation is low.

**What this dashboard addresses:**
The Weekly Reset Routine is designed specifically for this. It is a single repeatable structure — 15 minutes, once per week — that maintains momentum without requiring high effort. The Momentum Tracker reinforces consistency by making progress visible.

**Mark this as a priority if:**
You have restarted financial plans multiple times, feel like you "fall off track" regularly, or struggle to maintain financial habits beyond the first few weeks.

---

#### Priority 4 — Debt Pressure

**What it means:**
Debt is creating a consistent weight on your financial decisions and emotional state. It may be limiting your ability to save, restricting your options, or creating a background anxiety that makes it hard to think clearly about other financial priorities.

**Why it creates pressure:**
Debt pressure is both financial and psychological. The financial cost is real — interest compounds, options narrow, and the margin for error shrinks. But the psychological cost is often equally damaging: avoidance, shame, and a sense that getting ahead is impossible. Both need to be addressed structurally.

**What this dashboard addresses:**
The Priority Structure System organizes your financial obligations into a clear hierarchy — survival, stabilization, and growth — so debt is addressed in the right order without neglecting other critical areas. The Financial Reset Workbook includes a debt visibility exercise that brings everything into one honest picture.

**Mark this as a priority if:**
Debt payments are your most consistent source of financial stress, you avoid looking at your full debt picture, or you feel like debt is preventing you from making real financial progress.

---

#### Priority 5 — Income Instability

**What it means:**
Your income varies — month to month, season to season, or project to project. This unpredictability makes budgeting difficult, saving inconsistent, and financial planning feel nearly impossible. The irregular nature of your income may be creating a feast-or-famine pattern that is hard to break.

**Why it creates pressure:**
Variable income requires a different financial architecture than stable income. Most conventional financial advice is built for predictable paychecks. When income is irregular, the systems need to be designed around that reality — with buffer accounts, flexible allocation models, and priority sequencing that adjusts to what's actually available each month.

**What this dashboard addresses:**
The Priority Structure System is especially important for variable income situations — it provides a sequenced framework for allocating money regardless of how much arrives. The Opportunity Expansion System addresses income growth and stabilization directly.

**Mark this as a priority if:**
Your income varies significantly from month to month, you work freelance, contract, or commission-based, or you feel like budgeting is impossible because you never know what's coming in.

## SECTION 3 — YOUR 4-WEEK RESET ROADMAP

*Placement: Second full section. Follows Priority Areas.*

---

### Section Header

**Your 4-Week Reset Roadmap**

### Purpose Statement

> This is a structured sequence — not a curriculum. Each week builds on the last. You are not meant to complete everything in a week. You are meant to do the three actions assigned to that week, mark them complete, and move forward. Consistency across four weeks builds more momentum than intensity in one.

*Design note: Display as a horizontal timeline or vertical accordion. Each week collapses and expands. Completed weeks should show a visual marker — not a dramatic celebration, just a quiet confirmation that it's done.*

---

### Week 1 — Stabilize

**Theme:** Reduce pressure and confusion before building anything new.

**Focus:** Visibility. Before you can organize your finances, you need to see them clearly — all of them, at the same time. This week is not about fixing anything. It is about looking honestly.

**The 3 Actions:**

1. **Complete the Financial Visibility Exercise** in your Financial Reset Workbook. List every financial obligation, income source, account, and subscription you currently have. Everything in one place, at the same time. Do not skip anything because it feels uncomfortable.

2. **Identify your single highest-pressure area.** Based on what you saw in the visibility exercise, name the one area creating the most stress right now. Write it down. This becomes your anchor for Week 2.

3. **Set up your Weekly Reset time.** Choose a specific day and 15-minute window each week that you will dedicate to your Weekly Reset Routine. Put it in your calendar as a recurring event. This is your most important structural decision this week.

**Completion Marker:**
> Week 1 is complete when you have looked at everything, named your highest-pressure area, and scheduled your weekly reset time.

---

### Week 2 — Organize

**Theme:** Build visibility and structure into your financial life.

**Focus:** Architecture. Now that you can see your full financial picture, this week is about giving everything a place. Organized money behaves differently than scattered money — not because the amounts changed, but because clarity changes how you make decisions.

**The 3 Actions:**

1. **Complete the Bill Organization Template.** List every recurring expense with its due date, amount, and payment method. Identify any that can be consolidated, automated, or eliminated. This document becomes your financial infrastructure reference.

2. **Set up your Financial Calendar.** Map every bill due date, income date, and financial obligation into a single monthly calendar view. Use the Financial Calendar template. This turns your financial life from reactive to visible.

3. **Define your three financial priorities using the Priority Structure System.** Based on your current situation, identify what falls into each category: survival (non-negotiable obligations), stabilization (reducing pressure), and growth (building toward something). This framework determines how you allocate money for the rest of the program.

**Completion Marker:**
> Week 2 is complete when every bill has a place on your calendar, your priorities are defined, and your financial architecture is documented.

---

### Week 3 — Simplify

**Theme:** Create repeatable systems that run without full effort.

**Focus:** Reduction. Complexity is the enemy of consistency. This week is about removing the friction that makes financial management feel hard. The goal is not a perfect system — it is a simple one that you can actually maintain.

**The 3 Actions:**

1. **Implement the Decision Pause System.** Using the Decision Filter Worksheet, define your personal spending filter — the three questions you will ask before any unplanned purchase over a threshold you set. Write it down. Put it somewhere visible. The system only works if it interrupts the decision in the moment.

2. **Identify two things to automate.** Review your Bill Organization Template and choose two payments or transfers that can run automatically. Automation removes decisions from your weekly mental load. Start small — two automations that work reliably are worth more than ten that don't.

3. **Complete your first Weekly Reset.** Using the Weekly Reset Sheet, work through your first structured financial review. It should take 15 minutes or less. Note what you spent, what you planned, where there's a gap, and what you're adjusting. This is the routine that holds everything together.

**Completion Marker:**
> Week 3 is complete when your decision filter is written down, two things are automated, and you have completed your first Weekly Reset.

---

### Week 4 — Execute

**Theme:** Build consistency and momentum.

**Focus:** Rhythm. The first three weeks built the structure. This week is about proving to yourself that you can sustain it. Execution is not about doing more — it is about repeating what works. Momentum comes from consistency, not intensity.

**The 3 Actions:**

1. **Complete your second Weekly Reset.** The goal is repetition. The Weekly Reset Routine only becomes a system after you have done it more than once. Notice what feels easier this time. That ease is the system beginning to hold.

2. **Review the Opportunity Expansion System.** Using the framework, identify one realistic income possibility and one financial friction point you can reduce in the next 30 days. Write both down. This is not about radical change — it is about identifying your next move before you need it.

3. **Complete your Monthly Review using the Monthly Review Sheet.** Look at the full four weeks: what shifted, what stayed the same, where you gained clarity, and what your next priority is. This document becomes your baseline for the next 30 days.

**Completion Marker:**
> Week 4 is complete when you have run your second Weekly Reset, identified your next move, and completed your Monthly Review. You have now built a financial operating system. Use it.

## SECTION 4 — SYSTEM BUILDERS

*Placement: Third full section. Follows the 4-Week Roadmap.*

---

### Section Header

**System Builders**

### Purpose Statement

> These are the five core systems of the ETFM Financial Reset. Each one is designed to replace a specific pattern with a specific structure. They work independently — but they compound when used together. Build them in order the first time. After that, return to the one that needs the most attention.

*Design note: Display as expandable cards. Each system should feel like a tool you pick up and use — not a lesson you read and absorb. Lead with the deliverable, not the concept.*

---

### System 1 — Weekly Financial Awareness System

**What it is:**
A 15-minute weekly review structure that creates consistent visibility into your financial life. Not a budget audit. Not a full accounting. A brief, structured check-in that keeps you from losing track of where things stand.

**Why it matters:**
Most financial problems are not caused by a lack of knowledge — they are caused by a lack of awareness. When you stop paying attention, money behaves according to its defaults, which are almost never aligned with your intentions. The Weekly Financial Awareness System is the mechanism that keeps you paying attention without making it a significant time commitment.

**How to implement:**
1. Choose a recurring day and time — 15 minutes, once per week. Non-negotiable. Put it in your calendar.
2. Open your Weekly Reset Sheet at the start of every session.
3. Work through the four standard questions: What came in this week? What went out? Where is the gap? What am I adjusting?
4. Note one thing that worked and one thing to watch next week.
5. Close the sheet. You are done.

**The deliverable:**
A completed Weekly Reset Sheet for each week. Over time, this becomes a record of your financial patterns — not just your transactions.

**Tools it uses:** Weekly Reset Sheet, Habit Tracker

---

### System 2 — Decision Pause System

**What it is:**
A simple, pre-committed filter that interrupts unplanned and emotionally-driven spending before it happens. Not a restriction. Not a budget rule. A structured pause that creates space between the impulse and the decision.

**Why it matters:**
Reactive spending is structural, not moral. It happens because the default path — buy now, evaluate later — requires no friction. The Decision Pause System introduces one moment of friction at exactly the right point: before the purchase, not after. That single moment is where financial behavior actually changes.

**How to implement:**
1. Using the Decision Filter Worksheet, set your personal threshold — the dollar amount above which you will pause before purchasing.
2. Write your three filter questions. Suggested defaults: Is this planned? Can I replace something else with this? Will I value this in 72 hours?
3. Keep the filter visible — phone notes, a card in your wallet, wherever you make decisions.
4. When you hit the threshold, pause. Run the filter. Then decide.
5. The filter does not tell you not to buy. It tells you to decide — intentionally.

**The deliverable:**
A completed Decision Filter Worksheet with your threshold, your three questions, and your personal commitment written down.

**Tools it uses:** Decision Filter Worksheet

---

### System 3 — Priority Structure System

**What it is:**
A framework that organizes every financial obligation and goal into three clear tiers — survival, stabilization, and growth — and determines how money gets allocated based on those priorities. Not a budget. A hierarchy.

**Why it matters:**
When everything feels equally urgent, nothing gets done strategically. Most financial stress comes not from a lack of money but from a lack of clarity about what to do with it. The Priority Structure System removes the ambiguity. It tells you exactly what to pay first, what to stabilize second, and what to build toward third — based on your actual situation, not a generic formula.

**How to implement:**
1. Using the Priority Planner, list every financial obligation and goal you currently have.
2. Sort each item into one of three tiers:
   - **Survival:** Non-negotiable. Housing, food, utilities, essential transportation. These come first, always.
   - **Stabilization:** Reducing pressure. Minimum debt payments, emergency buffer, critical recurring costs. These come second.
   - **Growth:** Building forward. Savings goals, debt acceleration, investment contributions. These come third.
3. Allocate available income in tier order. Growth only happens after survival and stabilization are covered.
4. Review and adjust monthly using your Monthly Review Sheet.

**The deliverable:**
A completed Priority Planner with every financial obligation and goal tiered and allocated.

**Tools it uses:** Priority Planner, Financial Reset Workbook, Monthly Review Sheet

---

### System 4 — Weekly Reset Routine

**What it is:**
A single, repeatable weekly structure that maintains financial momentum without requiring significant time, energy, or willpower. Fifteen minutes. One day per week. The same structure, every time.

**Why it matters:**
Most financial plans fail not because they are wrong but because they require too much effort to maintain. The Weekly Reset Routine is designed to be the minimum viable financial habit — the one thing that, if done consistently, keeps everything else from drifting. It does not require you to be disciplined every day. It requires you to show up for 15 minutes, once a week, and do the same thing you did last week.

**How to implement:**
1. Designate your reset day — the same day each week. Sunday evening and Monday morning are common choices. Choose what fits your rhythm.
2. Open your Weekly Reset Sheet.
3. Work through the four standard questions (in, out, gap, adjustment).
4. Review your Priority Planner to confirm your allocation is still accurate.
5. Note one momentum marker — something you did that was financially intentional this week.
6. Close everything. Done.

**The standard questions:**
- What came in this week?
- What went out?
- Where is the gap between what I planned and what happened?
- What am I adjusting for next week?

**The deliverable:**
A completed Weekly Reset Sheet for every week. Consistency over time is the product.

**Tools it uses:** Weekly Reset Sheet, Priority Planner, Habit Tracker

---

### System 5 — Opportunity Expansion System

**What it is:**
A structured framework for identifying realistic income possibilities and financial friction points specific to your current situation — your skills, your schedule, your obligations, and your market. Not generic hustle advice. A methodical approach to finding your next financial move.

**Why it matters:**
Most people approach income growth reactively — they think about it when money is tight and stop thinking about it when things stabilize. The Opportunity Expansion System makes it structural. It builds a map of what is actually available to you, identifies where friction is costing you money you already have, and creates a clear next step rather than a vague intention.

**How to implement:**
1. Open the Execution Scorecard to the Opportunity Expansion section.
2. Work through the two-part framework:

   **Part 1 — Income Possibilities:** List every realistic income opportunity available to you in the next 90 days. Not aspirational. Realistic. Consider existing skills, available time, current relationships, and market access. Identify the highest-leverage option and define one concrete next step.

   **Part 2 — Friction Reduction:** List every recurring cost, fee, subscription, or financial habit that is not serving your priorities. Identify the top three to address. Friction reduction is income — it costs nothing and produces immediate results.

3. Choose one item from each part to pursue in the next 30 days.
4. Review monthly using your Monthly Review Sheet.

**The deliverable:**
A completed Opportunity Expansion section in your Execution Scorecard with one income action and one friction reduction identified and committed to.

**Tools it uses:** Execution Scorecard, Monthly Review Sheet, Priority Planner

## IMPLEMENTATION NOTES FOR DASHBOARD BUILD

*These notes are for the developer or platform designer, not for display to the client.*

---

**Navigation:**
- The Quick Action Bar (Start Here, Open Week 1, Downloads & Tools, Track Momentum) should be sticky — always visible as the client scrolls.
- Sections should be accessible by anchor link from the navigation bar.
- Mobile layout should stack sections vertically and maintain the sticky nav.

**Priority Areas — Personalization Logic:**
- When a client marks a Priority Area, surface related tools (highlighted or tagged) in the Downloads & Tools section.
- This does not require a backend — a simple toggle with a CSS class change is sufficient.
- Do not hide unrelated tools. Surface the relevant ones first.

**Roadmap — Progress State:**
- Each week should have an "open" and "complete" state.
- Completed weeks should visually indicate completion — muted background, checkmark, subtle treatment.
- Do not lock future weeks. Clients may need to move non-linearly.

**Momentum Tracker:**
- Weekly grid should persist state — use localStorage or a simple backend if available.
- Reflective prompts should be text input fields, client-side only. Do not submit or store this data externally.
- Milestones should trigger on completion of specific actions, not on a time basis.

**Downloads & Tools:**
- Each tool should have a clear open/download button.
- If tools are hosted as Google Docs or Notion pages, open in a new tab.
- If tools are PDFs, download directly.
- Include a "last updated" date stamp on each tool so clients know the version they are using.

**Visual Design Principles:**
- Background: off-white (#f7f4ef) or dark (#1a1a2e) — not pure white or pure black.
- Accent: gold (#c9973a) used sparingly — for labels, dividers, and key actions only.
- Typography: serif for headlines (Georgia or equivalent), sans-serif for body.
- Spacing: generous. Let sections breathe. Density creates anxiety.
- Completion states: quiet and affirming. Not celebratory.
